EQUITY RESEARCH AGENT KNOWLEDGE
01 — COMPANY ANALYSIS AND FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

PURPOSE

This document provides foundational guidance for analyzing a public company.

The analysis should understand the company's business model, financial performance, financial position, industry, competitors, capital allocation, and economic drivers.

1. COMPANY ANALYSIS WORKFLOW

A professional company-analysis process should:

* Understand the issuer's business model.
* Analyze historical financial performance.
* Analyze the company's financial position.
* Analyze the industry and competitors.
* Identify revenue and profitability drivers.
* Assess working capital and cash generation.
* Assess capital investment and capital structure.
* Forecast future financial performance.
* Perform valuation.
* Identify investment risks and uncertainties.
* Communicate conclusions using clear evidence and assumptions.

2. BUSINESS MODEL

Analyze:

* Products and services.
* Customers and customer concentration.
* Revenue sources.
* Geographic exposure.
* Distribution channels.
* Pricing model.
* Cost structure.
* Capital intensity.
* Competitive advantages.
* Capital allocation.

3. REVENUE ANALYSIS

Where appropriate, decompose revenue using operating drivers.

Examples:

Revenue = Volume × Price

Revenue may also be analyzed by:

* Product.
* Segment.
* Geography.
* Customer.
* Channel.
* Subscription base.
* Market share.

Use company disclosures whenever possible.

4. PRICING POWER

Pricing power is the ability to increase prices without causing an unacceptable loss of demand.

Assess:

* Industry structure.
* Competitive intensity.
* Product differentiation.
* Switching costs.
* Brand strength.
* Customer concentration.
* Regulation.
* Cost inflation.
* Ability to pass costs to customers.

5. COST AND PROFITABILITY ANALYSIS

Analyze:

* Gross profit.
* Gross margin.
* Operating income.
* Operating margin.
* Net income.
* Net margin.
* Fixed versus variable costs where information permits.
* Operating leverage.
* Financial leverage.
* Working-capital management.

Operating leverage describes the sensitivity of operating profit to changes in sales.

Financial leverage describes the sensitivity of equity earnings to changes in operating performance and financing structure.

6. FINANCIAL POSITION

Review:

* Cash and cash equivalents.
* Debt.
* Net debt.
* Liquidity.
* Working capital.
* Shareholders' equity.
* Capital expenditures.
* Debt maturities.
* Refinancing requirements.
* Material contingent obligations.
* Material off-balance-sheet arrangements where applicable.

7. CASH FLOW

Assess:

* Operating cash flow.
* Capital expenditure.
* Free cash flow.
* Working-capital effects.
* Sustainability of cash generation.
* Differences between accounting earnings and cash generation.

Do not assume accounting earnings automatically equal economic cash generation.

8. FINANCIAL RATIOS

Useful categories include:

* Liquidity ratios.
* Profitability ratios.
* Leverage and solvency ratios.
* Activity and efficiency ratios.
* Return ratios.
* Valuation ratios.

Ratios should be interpreted:

* Across time.
* Against appropriate peers.
* Against industry economics.
* In the context of the company's business model.
* With attention to accounting differences.

Never compare ratios mechanically when accounting policies or business models make the comparison misleading.

9. EARNINGS QUALITY

Investigate:

* Nonrecurring items.
* Unusual gains or losses.
* Revenue-recognition issues.
* Changes in accounting estimates.
* Working-capital movements.
* Stock-based compensation.
* Acquisition effects.
* Restructuring.
* Margin changes.
* Cash conversion.

10. COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

Compare economically similar peers on:

* Revenue growth.
* Margins.
* Profitability.
* Cash generation.
* Leverage.
* Returns.
* Valuation.
* Market position.
* Business-model characteristics.

Peer selection must be economically justified.

11. EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE

Use these labels when appropriate:

FACT
Directly supported by a reliable source.

CALCULATION
Produced by a deterministic formula or calculation tool.

ASSUMPTION
An explicit analyst/model input.

INTERPRETATION
An analytical conclusion derived from evidence.

OPINION
A judgment that goes beyond directly observed facts.

Never present an assumption as a fact.

12. SOURCE PRIORITY

Prefer:

1. Regulatory and government sources.
2. Audited financial statements.
3. Official company disclosures.
4. Official industry/regulatory sources.
5. Reputable market-data providers.
6. Reputable secondary research.

Search-engine snippets are discovery aids, not evidence.
