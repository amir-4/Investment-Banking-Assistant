EQUITY RESEARCH AGENT KNOWLEDGE
02 — FORECASTING AND SCENARIO ANALYSIS

PURPOSE

Forecasting is a core equity-research activity used to evaluate future company performance and support valuation.

1. FORECASTING PRINCIPLES

A forecast should:

* Have a defined forecast horizon.
* Identify the economic and operating drivers.
* Use historical evidence where relevant.
* Incorporate current company disclosures.
* Incorporate relevant industry information.
* State assumptions explicitly.
* Be internally consistent.
* Preserve uncertainty when evidence is weak.

2. FORECAST OBJECTS

Potential forecast objects include:

* Revenue.
* Operating expenses.
* Margins.
* Working capital.
* Capital expenditure.
* Depreciation.
* Debt.
* Cash.
* Free cash flow.
* Other company-specific financial drivers.

Select forecast variables based on:

* Information availability.
* Relevance.
* Accuracy.
* Explanatory value.
* Verifiability.

3. TOP-DOWN FORECASTING

A top-down approach can move through:

Macroeconomy → Industry → Company → Financial Statements

Potential inputs include:

* GDP growth.
* Interest rates.
* Inflation.
* Commodity prices.
* Industry growth.
* Market size.
* Market share.
* Regulatory changes.

4. BOTTOM-UP FORECASTING

A bottom-up approach begins with company-level operating drivers.

Examples:

Revenue = Units Sold × Average Price

Other examples:

* Customers × Revenue per Customer.
* Subscribers × ARPU.
* Stores × Sales per Store.
* Transactions × Revenue per Transaction.

5. REVENUE FORECASTING

Consider:

* Volume.
* Price.
* Product mix.
* Geographic mix.
* Customer growth.
* Market share.
* Foreign-exchange effects.
* Acquisitions.
* Divestitures.
* Management guidance.

6. OPERATING EXPENSE FORECASTING

Consider:

* Historical margins.
* Fixed versus variable costs.
* Management guidance.
* Headcount.
* Capacity.
* Input costs.
* Operating leverage.
* One-time costs.

7. WORKING CAPITAL

Consider:

* Accounts receivable.
* Inventory.
* Accounts payable.
* Revenue growth.
* Cost growth.
* Historical turnover.
* Days metrics.
* Industry norms.

8. CAPITAL INVESTMENT

Forecast:

* Maintenance CapEx.
* Growth CapEx.
* Expansion requirements.
* Capacity additions.
* Technology investment.
* Depreciation where appropriate.

Do not assume capital expenditure requirements are economically identical across companies.

9. CAPITAL STRUCTURE

Consider:

* Debt levels.
* Debt maturities.
* Refinancing requirements.
* Cash balances.
* Share repurchases.
* Equity issuance.
* Acquisition financing.
* Capital-allocation policy.

10. SCENARIO ANALYSIS

When uncertainty is material, create:

BEAR CASE

BASE CASE

BULL CASE

Each scenario should specify:

* Key assumptions.
* Main operating drivers.
* Expected financial outcomes.
* Valuation implications.
* Major risks.

11. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS

Sensitivity analysis measures how changes in assumptions affect an output.

Common DCF sensitivities include:

* WACC.
* Terminal growth.
* Revenue growth.
* Operating margin.
* EBITDA margin.
* Capital expenditure.
* Working capital.

Common relative-valuation sensitivities include:

* Peer selection.
* Median versus mean multiple.
* Target-company growth.
* Margin differences.
* Business-model differences.

12. FORECAST DISCIPLINE

Never present a forecast as a fact.

Use explicit terminology such as:

"Historical fact..."

"Management guidance indicates..."

"Analyst estimate..."

"Base-case assumption..."

"Bear-case assumption..."

"Bull-case assumption..."

13. CONFLICTING INFORMATION

If company guidance, historical data, or external estimates conflict:

* Identify the disagreement.
* Compare source quality.
* Compare dates.
* Compare reporting periods.
* Explain the selected assumption.
* Preserve uncertainty if it cannot be resolved.

14. ESCALATION

Escalate when:

* Material information is missing.
* Sources materially conflict.
* Forecasts depend on unsupported assumptions.
* Potential material non-public information is involved.
* The requested forecast requires regulated investment advice.
