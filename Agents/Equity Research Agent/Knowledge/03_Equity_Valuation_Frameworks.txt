EQUITY RESEARCH AGENT KNOWLEDGE
03 — EQUITY VALUATION FRAMEWORKS

PURPOSE

This document defines professional valuation concepts and methodology for equity research.

1. VALUATION PROCESS

Use the following sequence:

1. Understand the business.
2. Understand the company's economic drivers.
3. Forecast company performance.
4. Select an appropriate valuation model.
5. Calculate valuation.
6. Perform sensitivity/scenario analysis where material.
7. Interpret the results.
8. Communicate limitations.

The appropriate valuation model depends on:

* Company characteristics.
* Business model.
* Data availability.
* Forecastability.
* Capital structure.
* Valuation purpose.

2. INTRINSIC VALUATION

Intrinsic valuation estimates value from the economic characteristics of the asset.

Important approaches include:

* Discounted cash flow.
* Free cash flow to the firm.
* Free cash flow to equity.
* Dividend discount models.
* Residual income.

3. DCF / FCFF

A simplified FCFF DCF process is:

1. Forecast FCFF.
2. Discount forecast FCFF using WACC.
3. Estimate terminal value.
4. Discount terminal value.
5. Calculate enterprise/firm value.
6. Bridge enterprise value to equity value using appropriate balance-sheet adjustments.

Perpetuity-growth terminal value:

Terminal Value = FCFF(n+1) / (WACC - g)

Where:

FCFF(n+1) = FCFF in the first terminal-period year.

WACC = Weighted Average Cost of Capital.

g = Long-term terminal growth rate.

Important:

* WACC must be greater than terminal growth for this formula.
* Terminal growth must be economically defensible.
* Terminal assumptions should be consistent with long-term economic conditions.
* DCF valuation is highly sensitive to assumptions.
* Sensitivity analysis should be used when assumptions are material.

4. WACC

For a simplified debt/equity structure:

WACC = We × Re + Wd × Rd × (1 - Tax Rate)

Where:

We = Market-value weight of equity.

Wd = Market-value weight of debt.

Re = Cost of equity.

Rd = Pre-tax cost of debt.

Tax Rate = Applicable tax rate.

If preferred equity is material, incorporate its market-value weight and required return.

Never invent:

* Market value of equity.
* Market value of debt.
* Cost of equity.
* Cost of debt.
* Tax rate.

Inputs must be sourced or explicitly modeled.

5. FCFE

FCFE estimates cash flow available to common equity holders after considering required investment and debt-related cash flows.

Use when an equity-cash-flow approach is appropriate for the company's characteristics and capital structure.

6. DIVIDEND DISCOUNT MODEL

DDM values equity based on expected future dividends.

It can be appropriate when:

* Dividends are meaningful.
* Dividend policy is relatively stable.
* Dividends reasonably represent distributable cash flows.

Do not use DDM mechanically when dividends do not represent the company's economic cash-flow capacity.

7. RELATIVE VALUATION

Common price multiples:

* P/E.
* P/B.
* P/S.
* Price/Operating Cash Flow.

Common enterprise-value multiples:

* EV/EBITDA.
* EV/Sales.
* Other operating multiples when economically appropriate.

8. COMPARABLE COMPANY ANALYSIS

Peer analysis should:

* Select economically comparable companies.
* Use consistent reporting periods.
* Normalize definitions where appropriate.
* Consider growth.
* Consider margins.
* Consider risk.
* Consider capital intensity.
* Consider capital structure.
* Consider business-model differences.

Use median and distribution information rather than relying only on the mean.

9. MULTIPLE SELECTION

Do not select a valuation multiple only because it produces a favorable valuation.

Explain why the selected multiple is appropriate.

10. MULTI-METHOD VALUATION

Use multiple valuation methods when appropriate.

Potential methods:

* DCF.
* Trading comparables.
* Dividend discount.
* Residual income.
* Other company-specific approaches.

Differences between methods are information and should be explained.

11. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS

For DCF consider:

* WACC.
* Terminal growth.
* Revenue growth.
* Margins.
* CapEx.
* Working capital.

For comparable valuation consider:

* Peer selection.
* Median versus mean.
* Metric definition.
* Target-company differences.

12. VALUATION BOUNDARY

A valuation is an estimate.

It is NOT:

* A guaranteed future market price.
* A guaranteed return.
* A guaranteed investment outcome.

Never communicate valuation certainty when the underlying assumptions are uncertain.

13. TOOL USAGE

Use deterministic tools for mathematical calculations:

* calculate_wacc
* calculate_dcf
* calculate_comparable_multiples

Do not manually invent calculator outputs.
