# Market access risk controls reference (SEC Rule 15c3-5)

Source: SEC Rule 15c3-5 (17 CFR 240.15c3-5), the SEC's Small Entity
Compliance Guide, and SEC staff FAQs on the rule. This governs any
broker-dealer providing market access — including via automated/
algorithmic systems — and directly shapes what this agent must never do.

## 1. What the rule requires

Broker-dealers with market access must establish, document, and maintain
risk management controls and supervisory procedures reasonably designed
to systematically limit financial exposure and ensure regulatory
compliance. The rule applies to ALL orders — whether entered manually by
a trader or generated automatically by a pre-programmed system. It
effectively eliminated "unfiltered" or "naked" access to an exchange or
ATS.

## 2. The required pre-trade controls (the core of what this agent enforces)

The risk management controls must be reasonably designed to:

1. **Prevent orders that exceed pre-set credit or capital thresholds** —
   this is the financial-exposure control most directly relevant to this
   agent: a hard credit/capital limit that must never be silently
   exceeded.
2. **Prevent orders in securities a person is restricted from trading** —
   a regulatory/compliance-list control.
3. **Restrict access to trading systems and technology to pre-approved,
   authorized persons and accounts.**
4. **Assure appropriate surveillance personnel receive immediate
   post-trade execution reports** — meaning breaches and executions must
   be surfaced promptly, not batched or delayed.

## 3. Governance requirements around the controls

- The controls must be under the **direct and exclusive control** of the
  broker-dealer itself (with narrow, defined exceptions) — they cannot
  be outsourced to a customer or another party without meeting specific
  conditions.
- Controls must be reviewed regularly for effectiveness, with a
  documented annual business-activity review.
- The firm's CEO (or equivalent) must annually certify that the controls
  comply with the rule.
- The rule applies to quotes as well as orders — an automated
  quoting/market-making system must also be controlled to prevent
  inadvertent excessive quoting.

## 4. What this means operationally for trading_monitor_agent

- Any pre-set credit, capital, or position threshold the agent is told
  about is treated as hard and non-negotiable — the agent flags a
  breach or near-breach immediately rather than waiting for a batch
  report, consistent with the rule's "immediate" surveillance
  requirement.
- The agent never treats itself as authorized to loosen, override, or
  reinterpret a threshold — that authority sits with the humans
  responsible for the firm's risk controls (per the "direct and
  exclusive control" requirement).
- The agent should treat a restricted-list / trading-restriction flag on
  a security the same way: report it, do not attempt to route around it
  or assume it no longer applies.
