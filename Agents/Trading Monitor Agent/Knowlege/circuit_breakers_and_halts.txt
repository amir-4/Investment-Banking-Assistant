# Circuit breakers and trading halts reference

Source: Investor.gov (SEC's investor-education site), SEC white papers
on the LULD Plan, and NYSE/Nasdaq rule references (NYSE Rule 7.12,
Nasdaq Rule 4121). Two distinct mechanisms exist and must not be
conflated.

## 1. Market-wide circuit breakers (MWCB) — "is the whole market falling apart?"

Triggered by the S&P 500 index alone, and halt trading across every US
equity and equity-options exchange simultaneously. Three fixed
thresholds, calculated daily from the prior day's S&P 500 close:

| Level | Decline | Effect |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Level 1 | 7% | Halts market-wide trading for 15 minutes if triggered before 3:25pm ET; no halt if triggered at/after 3:25pm ET |
| Level 2 | 13% | Same as Level 1 |
| Level 3 | 20% | Halts market-wide trading for the remainder of the trading day, regardless of time triggered |

## 2. Limit Up-Limit Down (LULD) — "is this one stock printing prints that don't make sense?"

A single-security mechanism that prevents a stock from trading outside a
dynamic price band around a rolling reference price.

- Applies during regular trading hours, 9:30am-4:00pm ET.
- Price bands: 5% for Tier 1 NMS stocks (S&P 500, Russell 1000, and
  select ETPs) priced above $3; wider bands (up to 10-20%) for Tier 2
  and lower-priced securities; bands double during the last 25 minutes
  of the regular session for Tier 1 stocks and low-priced Tier 2 stocks.
- If price would move outside the band, the security enters a **Limit
  State**. If trading does not naturally return inside the band within
  15 seconds, a **5-minute trading pause** follows.
- LULD was made permanent by SEC approval in April 2019 and effectively
  replaced the earlier single-stock circuit breaker pilot.

## 3. What the agent must do when either mechanism triggers

- **Report, don't predict.** State plainly that a symbol has entered a
  Limit State, Straddle State, or trading pause, or that a market-wide
  breaker level has been hit. Do not guess when trading will resume or
  what price it will reopen at.
- **Flag order eligibility, not order fate.** During a halt, in-flight
  orders on the affected symbol(s) may not be eligible to execute —
  state this as a fact about eligibility, not as advice on what the
  trader should do next.
- **Distinguish the two mechanisms clearly** in any report: a single
  stock can be in an LULD pause while the broader market is nowhere near
  a market-wide breaker, and vice versa — never describe an LULD event
  as if it were a market-wide halt or imply market-wide risk from a
  single-name pause.
