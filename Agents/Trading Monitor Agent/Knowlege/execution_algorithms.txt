# Execution algorithms reference

Standard institutional execution strategies the agent must recognize and
track. Sources: Perold (1988) on implementation shortfall (the original
academic definition, still the industry reference), and standard
sell-side execution-desk technical training material (used consistently
across CFI, Street of Walls-style, and practitioner references).

## 1. TWAP — Time-Weighted Average Price

Splits a total order into equal-sized slices executed at even time
intervals across a chosen window, regardless of market volume.

```
Slice size = Total order size / Number of intervals
```

- Simple and predictable, but ignores intraday volume patterns — it can
  under-trade during high-liquidity periods and over-trade during thin
  ones.
- Benchmark for performance: the time-weighted average price over the
  execution window.

## 2. VWAP — Volume-Weighted Average Price

Paces execution to match the market's actual volume distribution over
the day, so the trader's own volume-weighted average price tracks the
market's.

```
VWAP = sum(Price_i * Volume_i) / sum(Volume_i)
```

- Industry-standard benchmark for "did the desk trade at a fair average
  price relative to the day."
- Still a scheduled/passive strategy: it does not react to news or a
  sudden price move mid-day, it simply continues pacing to volume.

## 3. POV — Percentage of Volume (participation strategy)

Targets a fixed percentage of real-time market volume rather than a
fixed time schedule.

```
Target execution rate = Participation rate x Real-time market volume
```

- Adaptive: trades faster when the market is more active, slower when
  it's quiet — this keeps the order's footprint proportional to actual
  liquidity.
- Trade-off: because it reacts to market volume, the total time to
  complete the order is not fixed and depends on how much volume
  actually occurs.

## 4. Implementation shortfall (IS)

Defined by Perold (1988) as the difference between the return on a
theoretical "paper" portfolio (executed instantly at the decision price)
and the actual portfolio as executed. It captures the full real-world
cost of trading:

```
Implementation shortfall = Decision-price cost
                          + Market impact cost
                          + Timing/delay cost
                          + Opportunity cost (unfilled portion)
```

- More aggressive than VWAP/TWAP by design: it explicitly balances the
  risk of trading too fast (market impact) against the risk of trading
  too slow (price drifting away — timing risk), rather than passively
  following a schedule.
- Used when a trader has higher conviction or urgency and is willing to
  accept more market impact to reduce timing risk.

## 5. What the agent tracks for each strategy type

| Strategy | Agent must report                                          |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| TWAP     | Actual vs. scheduled slice completion, time-weighted price variance |
| VWAP     | Running VWAP vs. market VWAP, participation pace vs. market volume |
| POV      | Actual participation rate vs. target rate, projected completion time |
| IS       | Running cost vs. decision/arrival price, split into impact vs. timing cost where data allows |
